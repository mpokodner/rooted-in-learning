# The Rooted Learner — Design Direction & Asset Briefs

Practical design guidance for real assets. The goal is a visual brand that feels rooted, rigorous, and calm — never earthy-cutesy, never corporate-cold — and resources that are low-cognitive-load for a teacher scanning them at 6 a.m.

Quick-use instruction: Before making any asset, fill the Creative Brief (§9). Then apply the rules for that specific asset type (§7). Default to less — white space, one focal point, one job per page.

Status note on tokens: Michelle has not confirmed final colors/fonts in the source materials. Everything in §3-§4 is proposed — needs founder approval. Once confirmed, update this section and remove the flag.

## 1. The Intended Emotional Experience

When a teacher sees a Rooted Learner asset, they should feel: "This person gets it. This will make my day easier, not busier. This is serious about kids without being heavy."

Three feelings to design toward:

- **Rooted** — grounded, trustworthy, warm. Natural motifs used sparingly (the sprout), never a garden of clip art.
- **Rigorous** — clean, organized, credible. Looks like it belongs next to a real curriculum, not a Pinterest craft.
- **Accessible & calm** — generous space, clear hierarchy, nothing shouting. Calm is the accessibility feature.

## 2. Core Design Principles

1. **Clarity over decoration.** Every element earns its place or is cut.
2. **One focal point per asset.** The eye should know where to land in under a second.
3. **Rooted, not cutesy.** Warmth comes from color, type, and language — not from cartoon pencils or googly-eyed apples.
4. **Rigorous, not cold.** Structure and grid, softened by warmth and generous spacing.
5. **Low cognitive load.** If a teacher has to study the layout, it failed. See §8.
6. **Consistency = brand equity.** The product family should be recognizable across a TPT store thumbnail grid (§10).
7. **Print-first for classroom materials.** Assume grayscale printing, no bleed, standard margins.

## 3. Color — proposed, needs founder approval

A warm, grounded, credible palette. Proposed direction:

| Role | Proposed | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary (rooted green) | deep sage / forest, e.g. #2F5D50 | Headers, logo, primary buttons |
| Secondary (warm clay) | terracotta/clay, e.g. #C36F49 | Accents, highlights, one CTA |
| Warm neutral (paper) | soft cream, e.g. #F5F0E6 | Backgrounds |
| Ink | near-black warm gray, e.g. #242019 | Body text |
| Support (sky) | muted blue, e.g. #6E93A6 | Secondary tags, ML/language callouts |

Rules: two primary colors max per asset + neutrals. Reserve the clay accent for the single most important element. Never use pure black on pure white for large fills (too harsh for the calm feel — use warm ink on cream). All pairings must pass the contrast rules in §6.

## 4. Typography — proposed, needs founder approval

- **Display/headers:** a humanist serif or warm slab (grounded, credible, not corporate). Proposed: Fraunces or Source Serif.
- **Body/UI:** a clean, highly legible humanist sans. Proposed: Inter or Source Sans.
- **Student-facing materials:** the most legible option, generous size (see §6). Avoid tight tracking; avoid all-caps for anything a student reads for meaning.
- Two type families maximum. Weight and size create hierarchy — not a third font.

## 5. Icons, Illustration & Photography

- **Icons:** simple line icons, single weight, one accent color. The sprout is the signature mark — use it as a small anchor, not wallpaper.
- **Illustration:** restrained, flat, warm. No busy scenes. No stocky "diverse kids high-fiving" cliche.
- **Photography** (when used): real, warm, natural light; classrooms and materials, not staged corporate smiles. Faces of identifiable students require permission — route to rooted-learner-legal. Prefer materials/hands/text over student faces to sidestep privacy entirely.

## 6. Accessibility Rules (non-negotiable)

- **Contrast:** body text ≥ 4.5:1; large text (≥24px/18pt bold) ≥ 3:1. Verify every color pairing; the calm palette must not sacrifice contrast.
- **Type size:** student-facing print ≥ 14pt body (larger for early grades); teacher-facing ≥ 11pt. On-screen social ≥ 16px equivalent; never put essential text below ~24px on a mobile graphic.
- **Hierarchy:** one clear H1 per asset, consistent heading steps, never signal meaning by color alone (pair with label or icon).
- **Print:** design to read in grayscale; test that tiers/levels are distinguishable without color. Standard margins, no essential content in the bleed zone.
- **Line length:** 50-75 characters for readable body blocks.

## 7. Rules by Asset Type

### a. TPT thumbnail (the storefront tile)
Legible at 2 inches wide on a phone. One clear title, one focal graphic, grade band visible.
Plain-language topic on the tile ("ELD Overlay: Narrative Writing, Gr 4-6") — not just the brand mark.
Consistent template so the store grid reads as a family (§10). See rooted-learner-tpt for conversion specifics.

### b. TPT preview (the multi-page walkthrough)
Page 1 = promise + what's included. Following pages = real interior shots (readable, not blurred teasers).
Show the teacher guide and one student page clearly. Honesty sells; no essential pieces hidden.
End page: brand mark, related products, terms-of-use note.

### c. Printable teacher/student materials
Maximum white space, one task per page, obvious where to write.
Grayscale-safe. One-click print (no assembly) unless assembly is the point.
Teacher directions visually separated from student content (e.g., a boxed header).
Student pages: generous type, clear numbering, predictable structure week to week.

### d. Social graphics (Instagram/short-video thumbnails)
One idea, big legible text, opens with the pain point.
4:5 or 1:1 for feed, 9:16 for reels/stories. Safe margins for captions/UI.
Brand mark small and consistent; never let it crowd the message.

### e. District one-pagers & slide decks
More systems-oriented, still warm. Grid-driven, lots of air.
One-pager: problem → what I do → proof → how to start, top to bottom, forwardable as a PDF.
Slides: one idea per slide, the locked template from weekly-microlesson-builder for microlessons.

## 8. Designing Low-Cognitive-Load Teacher Resources

Run every resource through this:

1. **Scan test:** can a teacher understand what to do in under 10 seconds of scanning?
2. **One job per page.** If a page does two things, split it.
3. **Predictable structure.** Same zones in the same places every time (title, teacher note, student work, reflection).
4. **Simplify before adding.** If a page feels dense, remove before you add. (Mirrors the ELD-overlay "simplify before adding" rule.)
5. **Reusability visible.** The teacher should see at a glance what they can reuse next week.

## 10. Product-Family System

So the ELD overlay line looks like one family across the TPT store:

- **Locked frame:** same thumbnail layout, same title zone, same color-role system, same sprout placement for every product in a line.
- **Variable by product:** the topic word, the grade band, and ONE color drawn from the approved palette to distinguish sub-lines (e.g., Reading vs. Writing overlays) — while keeping structure identical.
- **Naming shows the family:** "Rooted Reading," "Rooted Response," "Rooted ELD Overlays" as visible line names (see business-v2 brand architecture).
- **Result:** a curriculum director scanning the store sees a coherent system; a teacher sees a trustworthy, repeatable format.

## 11. "Do Not" Rules

- No clip-art cutesiness (no cartoon supplies, no comic-sans energy).
- No corporate-cold sterility (no stock handshakes, no gradient-tech look).
- No more than two type families or two primary colors per asset.
- No color-only meaning; no essential text in bleed; no sub-contrast pairings.
- No identifiable student faces without cleared permission (→ rooted-learner-legal).
- Don't invent "confirmed" brand tokens — keep §3-§4 flagged until Michelle approves.
