# The Rooted Learner — Business, Offer & Product Strategy (v2, current)

This supersedes rooted-learner-business. Where the older skill leads with district consulting, a bridge-year LinkedIn pipeline, and a four-line model, this v2 leads with the teacher/website/TPT lane. Michelle's stated direction: build website authority and subscribers, and make sales on TPT and the website. Consulting is real but becomes a warm, later lane fed by teacher-facing proof — not the near-term engine. When the two business skills conflict, v2 wins.

Quick-use instruction: Any growth question → ask "does this grow the list, grow website authority, or make a TPT/website sale?" If not, it's probably "not now" (§9). Keep the catalog tight, reuse one idea across channels, protect constrained founder time.

## 1. Company Identity & Strategic Frame

Founder: Michelle Marshall · Entity: The Rooted Learner LLC (Maryland) · Website: therootedlearner.com Stage: Full-time classroom teacher building a teacher-facing product + audience business alongside teaching. Founder time: treat business hours as real and finite. Evenings + weekends. Design every system to survive a heavy school week.

The business in one sentence (current): The Rooted Learner creates practical, standards-aligned ELD overlays and teacher-ready tools — sold direct to educators through TPT and the website — that make rigorous grade-level instruction accessible to multilingual learners without adding prep, while building the authority and audience that later open school/district work.

The moat: Michelle is a current classroom educator + WIDA/ELD-literate practitioner + builder (curriculum AND tech). Most TPT sellers aren't systems-literate; most consultants aren't in a classroom Monday. She is both. That shows up as resources that are genuinely low-prep AND genuinely rigorous.

Two connected lanes, one brand:

- **Lane A — Direct-to-teacher (PRIMARY):** TPT + website shop, free lead magnet, email list, blog, short videos.
- **Lane B — District/consulting (WARM/LATER):** audits, ELD overlay design, PD, custom tools. Fed by Lane A proof and LinkedIn.

They must reinforce, not confuse. A TPT download and a district engagement are different purchases with different language (see rooted-learner-brand §3).

## 2. Ideal Customer Profiles

**Lane A — Teachers (primary buyers)**

Grades ~3-8 ELA / reading interventionists / ESOL-ML teachers / MTSS.
Jobs-to-be-done: "Make tomorrow's grade-level text reachable for my newcomers without rewriting it." "Stop rebuilding scaffolds from scratch every Sunday." "Look credible and keep rigor high."
Pains: no prep time, generic resources that lower rigor, guilt over MLs falling behind, decision fatigue.
Desired outcomes: use it tomorrow, reuse it next week, kids access the real text.
Objections: "Will this actually be low-prep?" "Does it fit MY curriculum?" "Is it just repackaged worksheets?"
Purchase triggers: start of year, new newcomer mid-year, test season, a unit that's clearly too hard as-is.

**Lane B — District/school leaders (warm/later)**

Curriculum directors, Title III/EL coordinators, literacy directors, principals at high-ML schools.
JTBD: "Make our adopted curriculum work for MLs coherently, not teacher-by-teacher."
Pains: fragmented tools, ACCESS data that isn't moving, teacher workarounds, compliance pressure.
Objections: capacity, procurement, "is this a system or a workshop?"
Purchase triggers: new curriculum adoption, Title III planning, a flagged ML subgroup.

## 3. Offer Ladders

**Lane A — Teacher / TPT offer ladder**

1. **Free lead magnet** (email capture): one genuinely useful, complete resource — e.g., "MCAP-Style Constructed-Response Planner for Multilingual Learners" or "5 AI Workflows to Protect Rigor for MLs." A complete win, not a teaser.
2. **Entry single product ($6-9 on TPT):** "One Passage, Three Supports" — the recognizable "sample the method" product.
3. **Core single products ($18-28):** grade-specific ELD overlay / passage packs.
4. **Bundles ($ grade-band or seasonal):** collections of the above at a saved price.
5. **Website-only depth (later):** toolkit ($19-49), membership / generator access, mini-course. Sell access, system, and updates on the website — things a static PDF can't be.

**Lane B — District offer ladder (unchanged in shape, warm/later):**

Audit (clarity) → Build (ELD overlays, AI workflows, tools) → Implement (PD, coaching, iteration).
Enter through the Curriculum & Learning Systems Audit when a warm lead appears; don't cold-chase during the teacher-first phase.

Bridge between lanes: a curriculum director who buys a $24 overlay and finds it excellent is pre-sold on the quality of a Build. TPT is portfolio + proof, not just revenue.

## 4. TPT vs. Website — What Sells Where

| Sell on TPT | Sell on the website |
|---|---|
| Individual printable/digital overlay & passage sets | Subscription/membership access, generator credits |
| Single-topic packs, "try the method" starter kits | Full overlay module library, complete scope & sequence |
| Constructed-response organizers, ELD frames, vocab | Professional learning, coaching, school/district licenses |
| Seasonal/high-interest packs, planning templates | Custom work, audits, proof-of-concept, methodology/IP |

Guardrails (platform-safe): keep price consistency for any item listed on both platforms; don't undercut TPT on the website. Don't advertise off-TPT purchase links inside TPT products. Keep the generator's prompt architecture, full framework, editable masters, and complete scope & sequence website-only — sell finished outputs on TPT, never the engine. (Confirm specifics with rooted-learner-legal + TPT ToS.)

## 5. Product-Selection Rubric — build this first?

Score an idea before building (build the high scorers):

1. **Immediate use** — can a teacher use it tomorrow? (No → deprioritize.)
2. **Low prep** — passes the ELD-overlay teacher-time test?
3. **Rigor + access** — keeps grade level, adds access, no deficit framing?
4. **Reusable IP** — becomes a template/family, not a one-off?
5. **Search demand** — do teachers actually search for this? (→ rooted-learner-tpt.)
6. **Proof value** — would it impress a future district buyer?
7. **Time to build** — fits a constrained founder week?

Decision rule — product vs. bundle component vs. lead magnet vs. content vs. district POC:

- Solves one urgent problem, searchable, standalone value → **single product.**
- Only valuable alongside others / raises average order → **bundle component.**
- Best taste of the method, worth an email → **lead magnet.**
- Teaches/builds trust but isn't buyable → **blog/email/video content.**
- Too custom/systemic for a teacher download → **district proof-of-concept**, referenced not sold on TPT.

## 6. Pricing Principles

- Anchor to the ladder in §3; don't invent exact prices beyond documented ranges without founder sign-off.
- TPT sweet spots (from source): free lead magnet; $6-9 entry; $18-28 core; bundles priced below the sum.
- Website depth: entry $19-49; recurring $15-29/mo; premium modules/PD $149-499+ (from source materials — confirm before publishing).
- Bundle logic: bundle by grade band or by problem (assessment / ML support / AI planning), always cheaper than buying parts.
- Never undercut TPT on the website (platform rule). Never race to the bottom to "get the first sale."
- District pricing (Lane B, warm): Audit $3,500-$5,500; Build/Implement scale up. 50% deposit before work. PO billing available.

## 7. 90-Day Priority Framework (constrained founder time)

The order matters more than the volume. Ship the system, then feed it.

**Days 0-30 — Foundation & first sale**
- One lead magnet live + email capture on homepage/shop/blog.
- Stripe checkout on 1-2 flagship products; automated delivery.
- 5-email welcome sequence in Resend/React Email.
- One flagship TPT product family launched (entry + one core).
- Weekly newsletter template built; first 4 topics outlined.

**Days 31-60 — Authority & rhythm**
- Weekly newsletter cadence (70/30 value/promo).
- 2 blog posts/month, each with a content upgrade → list.
- 1 new product/month; first bundle when ≥3 related products exist.
- Short-video track begins (teacher how-to, tool-agnostic).

**Days 61-90 — Proof & compounding**
- First testimonials/reviews; first case-study-style email.
- Review the metrics (§8); double down on the one channel that's actually converting.
- Only now: if a warm district lead appears, offer the one-pager (Lane B).

## 8. Metrics That Change Decisions

- **TPT:** impressions → clicks → conversion per listing; best-seller; review count. Diagnose low-impression vs low-click vs low-conversion separately (→ rooted-learner-tpt).
- **Email/content:** list growth (net new), open/click, which lead magnet converts, which blog post drives signups (UTM-tagged).
- **Website:** sessions by source (GA4 + consistent UTMs), product conversion, revenue per visitor.
- **Lane B (later):** warm inbound count, fit calls, proposals, close rate.

Track only what changes a decision. Review monthly.

## 10. How TPT Intelligence Informs Consulting (ethically)

- Which overlays sell + which reviews recur = signal about the access problems teachers feel most — informs what districts likely need too.
- Use aggregate, anonymous patterns (search terms, best-sellers, review themes). Never treat individual teacher-buyers as research subjects, never quote a buyer without permission, never expose purchaser data (→ rooted-learner-legal).
- A proven best-selling overlay becomes a credible "here's what your teachers are already reaching for" artifact in a warm district conversation.
