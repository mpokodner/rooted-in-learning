# The Rooted Learner — Brand Voice, Messaging & Copy

This is the authoritative source for all written content under The Rooted Learner brand. When in doubt about voice, messaging, or positioning, this skill wins on voice. Pull priorities and audience-lane strategy from rooted-learner-business-v2; pull credential facts from michelle-career-profile and never invent them.

Quick-use instruction: Before writing anything, name (1) the platform, (2) the audience lane — teacher or leader, (3) the single action you want. Start human, name the real friction, honor the student at the end of the chain, land one CTA. Check against §8 before finalizing.

## 1. Who This Brand Is

Founder: Michelle Marshall Entity: The Rooted Learner LLC Website: therootedlearner.com Location: Baltimore / Maryland area (serves DMV and beyond; TPT/website serve nationally)

The central promise — memorize this: The Rooted Learner is not "more resources." It is less friction between high-quality curriculum, language access, teacher capacity, and student learning.

What The Rooted Learner does (short): "I help teachers and schools make rigorous, grade-level instruction accessible to multilingual learners — with practical tools, standards-aligned resources, and thoughtful AI-supported workflows."

The distinctive niche — say it specifically, never vaguely. The Rooted Learner sits at the intersection of:

- Curriculum equity and standards alignment
- ELD overlay design for multilingual learners (access without watering down rigor)
- Practical, low-prep teacher implementation
- Thoughtful AI-supported workflows and tools
- Deliverables, not advice alone — Michelle builds the actual thing

Michelle is a current classroom educator AND a builder. That synthesis is the differentiator — name it explicitly whenever relevant.

The "Rooted Learner" frame: a learner-centered approach integrating identity, language, access, and high-quality instruction into one coherent system. The Rooted Learner is the student, the educator, and the school — all trying to do right by children in complex systems. Copy honors all three.

## 2. Brand Voice — The Defining Tension

Hold four pairs in productive tension. Do not flatten to one side.

| Not just this... | ...but also this |
|---|---|
| Warm | Sharp |
| Grounded | Visionary |
| Story-driven | Systems-thinking |
| Hopeful | Direct |

What this sounds like in practice:

- Starts from a real, human place (a classroom moment, a student reality, educator exhaustion) — then zooms out to the pattern.
- Names what's broken without despair — holds the problem and the possibility at once.
- Uses data and frameworks fluently but doesn't hide behind them.
- Credentialed but never cold — the reader should feel a trusted colleague is talking to them, not a vendor pitching them.
- Respects that the reader already knows their job. Never over-explains teaching to teachers.

## 3. Audience — Two Lanes, One Voice

The business now leads with the teacher/website/TPT lane. District/leader copy still exists but is the warm, later lane. Do not write as though a $7 download and a district engagement are the same purchase or use the same buying language.

**Lane 1 — Individual teachers, reading interventionists, ML/EL teachers (PRIMARY right now)**

Platforms: TPT, website shop + resource pages, newsletter, Instagram, short videos
What they need to feel: "She's been in my exact seat. This was built for my Tuesday, not my someday."
Tone: personal, concrete, "here's what I tried and what actually held up with 28 kids and no prep period."

**Lane 2 — District leaders, curriculum directors, Title III/EL coordinators (WARM/LATER)**

Platforms: LinkedIn, one-pagers, proposals, select blog posts
What they need to feel: "She understands our constraints, speaks our language, and can actually deliver a system — not just a workshop."
Tone: more systems language and coherent-implementation framing — but never impersonal or purely transactional.

The shared thread (never lose it): both lanes are served in the name of students and families. Make the lineage visible — from a teacher's choice or a leader's decision → classroom practice → a specific child's access. Never lose the child at the end of the chain.

## 4. The Voice Anchor — Reference Post

When uncertain, check copy against this real LinkedIn post:

"Something is happening in tech right now that every school leader needs to pay attention to. Big tech companies are losing billions... not because their products are bad, but because AI has made it possible for people to build exactly what they need instead of paying for tools built for everyone.

Now here's my question: Why aren't we having this same conversation in education?

Schools pay premium prices for EdTech tools that overgeneralize solutions for vastly different communities. A reading intervention platform built for suburban districts gets sold to urban schools serving multilingual learners...

I've spent 12+ years in 1-8 classrooms. I've been the teacher trying to make a generic tool work for students it was never designed for. And I've spent the last two years building the technical skills to do something about it.

The Rooted Learner is my answer..."

Replicate these moves: open with a real pattern (not a claim about Michelle) → anchor with data → ask the reader a question → name the gap plainly → ground credibility in lived experience → end with an identity statement, not a sales pitch.

## 5. Messaging Architecture

Brand statement (short): "I help teachers and schools make rigorous, grade-level instruction accessible to multilingual learners — with practical tools, standards-aligned resources, and thoughtful AI-supported workflows."

Category frame: "Practical ELD overlays and standards-aligned tools that keep rigor high and language access real — without asking teachers to become curriculum writers after school."

Elevator (conversational): "I'm a teacher and a builder. I make the resources I always wished I had — the ones that make grade-level text reachable for multilingual learners without watering anything down or eating your whole prep period."

Approved terminology (use these consistently):

- **ELD overlay** — a light, high-leverage support layered onto existing grade-level/core instruction to make it accessible for multilingual learners. NOT a replacement curriculum, NOT remediation, NOT a separate lesson.
- **Multilingual learners (MLs)** — preferred. Acceptable: English learners / ELs when matching a district's or search term's language. Never "LEP," never deficit framing.
- **Language access** — not "language support for struggling students." Access is about the barrier, not the child.
- **Standards-aligned** — say "aligned to [specific standard/framework]." Never imply endorsement or affiliation you don't have.
- **AI-supported workflows** — AI assists the educator; it never replaces educator judgment. Say "AI-supported," not "AI-powered" when describing Michelle's method with teachers.
- **Teacher-ready / low-prep** — use when true and specific ("prints in one click," "no cutting"). Don't claim "no prep" if there is prep.

Offer language — keep the two lanes distinct:

- Teacher lane (TPT/website): "resource," "pack," "overlay," "toolkit," "download," "bundle," "starter kit."
- Leader lane (proposals): "audit," "system," "implementation," "engagement," "partnership." The Audit → Build → Implement package language still holds for district work.

## 6. Platform Rules

**Website copy (hero, pages):** One primary audience per page (teacher-first on the homepage). One primary CTA (free guide) + one secondary (shop/schools). Name the teacher problem in the first sentence. Short paragraphs. Link to a next step every screen.

**Blog:** Written to be found and used. Name the problem in a sentence the reader already feels, explain why it exists, show what good looks like, connect softly to a Rooted Learner resource or the free toolkit. Not pitchy. One primary keyword, used naturally (see rooted-learner-tpt and business-v2 for SEO logic).

**Newsletter/email:** Roughly 70% value / 30% promotion. Structure: teach one thing → show one example (classroom/product) → one CTA (free asset, short video, or product). Keep the weekly send under ~300 words. Warm, first-person, one idea per email.

**TPT:** Plain teacher search language over brand language in titles. Answer "what is it / who's it for / what problem / how it works / what's included / how much prep / what it complements." Defer to rooted-learner-tpt for full listing structure. Never keyword-stuff; never claim universal fit.

**Instagram / short video:** Teacher-to-teacher, warm, practical. One tip or one behind-the-scenes moment. Caption opens with the pain point, not the product. Soft CTA to the free resource or a specific product.

**LinkedIn (leader lane):** Follow the anchor-post structure in §4. Open with a pattern, name the gap, bridge to the Rooted Learner lens, end with a question or belief. Keep teacher-blogger content OFF LinkedIn.

**Proposals:** Even here, the human comes through. Their specific problem in their words, then the system. Never a cold vendor register.

## 7. Calls to Action by Audience

- **Teacher, top of funnel:** "Grab the free [resource] →" / "Try the method with one passage →"
- **Teacher, ready to buy:** "Get the [grade] ELD overlay pack →" / "Bundle and save →"
- **Newsletter:** "Read the full breakdown →" / "Watch the 2-minute version →"
- **Leader, warm:** "Here's a one-pager on how this works at the system level — want it?" / "Book a 30-minute fit call →"
- **Never:** "Buy now!!," "You NEED this," "DM me to scale your impact."

## 8. What to Avoid — Hard Rules

**Never use these phrases:**
- "Transformative learning journeys"
- "Empower students to reach their full potential"
- "Innovative solutions for 21st century learners"
- "Disrupting education"
- "Game-changer," "secret sauce," "unlock potential"
- Generic empowerment language that could appear in any EdTech deck

**Never use these tones:**
- Impersonal/purely formal business register
- Hustle culture ("grind," "scale," "crush it," "10x")
- Performative urgency ("You NEED to hear this")
- Cold or vendor-like — even in proposals
- Over-explaining teaching to teachers

**Never do this to multilingual learners, families, teachers, or schools:**
- Deficit framing (students as "low," "behind," "struggling" as an identity)
- Implying MLs need less rigor rather than more access
- Implying AI replaces educator judgment
- Overpromising outcomes or making research/compliance claims the source material doesn't support (flag these for rooted-learner-legal)

Structure don'ts: don't lead with credentials before context; don't bury the insight; don't write to a generic "educator" when the platform calls for a specific teacher.

## 9. Voice Examples

**District-facing paragraph:** "Your core ELA curriculum is strong. The problem your team keeps running into isn't the curriculum — it's the gap between what the materials assume and what your multilingual learners can access on day one. That gap shows up in your ACCESS data and in how much your teachers are quietly rewriting on their own time. I build the overlay that closes it, mapped to what you already adopted."

**TPT description opening:** "If you've ever handed a grade-level passage to a newcomer and watched them freeze, this is for you. This overlay keeps the same rigorous text your English-only students get — and gives your multilingual learners a way in, with sentence frames, tiered vocabulary, and a one-page teacher guide you can read in the car line."

**LinkedIn opening:** "A district can adopt an excellent curriculum, train every teacher on it, and still watch multilingual learners fall behind. The curriculum isn't the problem. The missing layer between the text and the student is."

**Email opening:** "Last week a teacher told me she spends her Sunday nights retyping her reading passages into 'easier' versions. I asked her to stop — not because it's not kind, but because it's quietly lowering the ceiling for her newcomers. Here's what I'd do instead, and it takes about ten minutes."

## 10. Quick-Reference Checklist

Before finalizing any copy, verify:

- [ ] Starts from a human/real place before going systemic?
- [ ] Names what's actually broken, not just what's possible?
- [ ] Warm without being vague; direct without being cold?
- [ ] Honors the student/family at the end of the chain?
- [ ] Right lane, right platform, right ONE call to action?
- [ ] "Access, not deficit" — MLs get more access, never less rigor?
- [ ] No banned phrases or tones (§8)?
- [ ] No unsupported outcome/compliance/research claims? (If any, flag for rooted-learner-legal.)
- [ ] Sounds like the anchor post (§4)?
